# lory.geometry: the shapes three.js builds

`lory.geometry` (packages/lory/geometry.co) makes the shapes three.js's
BufferGeometry classes make, laid out as three lays them out: the same
vertices in the same order, the same normals and uvs, the same triangles and
the same groups. A program draws them through `lory.space`, or reads their
numbers itself.

```
use lory.geometry

g = geometry.torus_knot[1.0, 0.4, 64, 8, 2.0, 3.0]
c = space.mesh[c, 'knot', geometry.space_shape[g]]
c = space.draw[c, 'knot', lory.identity[], space.plain[lory.hex['#e8590c']]]
c = geometry.lines_drawn[c, geometry.edges[g, 10.0], lory.identity[], 1.0, lory.white[]]
```

It is a reimplementation: no three.js or earcut source is included. Their
layouts and formulas were read from three 0.187.0 (earcut 3.0.2 within it)
and written anew. The one table taken as three has it is the order of the
regular polyhedra's faces; their corners are worked out in Lory. The header
of geometry.co carries the notice, in the words of packages/kite/NOTICE.

## What is built

### A geometry

| Function | What it is |
|---|---|
| `Geometry[positions, normals, uvs, indices, groups]` | Three numbers a vertex for its positions and normals, two for its uvs. `indices` are its triangles' corners, three a triangle (none where each three vertices in turn make one). `groups` are three numbers each: where the group starts, how many it counts, and its material. |
| `vertex_count[g]`, `triangle_count[g]` | How many vertices it holds and how many triangles it draws. |

### Shapes

Each takes three's arguments in three's order, with three's defaults.

| Function | What it is |
|---|---|
| `box[width, height, depth, width_segments, height_segments, depth_segments]` | Six faces (+x, -x, +y, -y, +z, -z), each a grid of its own two ways' cuts and a group of its own. |
| `plane[width, height, width_segments, height_segments]` | A grid facing +z, from its top left corner. |
| `circle[radius, segments, theta_start, theta_length]` | A fan about its centre, of at least 3 segments. |
| `ring[inner, outer, theta_segments, phi_segments, theta_start, theta_length]` | A flat ring, its rows' radii added up as three adds them. |
| `sphere[radius, width_segments, height_segments, phi_start, phi_length, theta_start, theta_length]` | Rows from the top down. A pole's fan has one triangle a segment, and its uvs are moved half a segment. |
| `cylinder[top, bottom, height, radial_segments, height_segments, open_ended, theta_start, theta_length]` | Its side (group 0), then its top (1) and its bottom (2) unless open ended. A radius of 0 has no cap and one triangle a segment where it narrows to a point. |
| `cone[radius, height, radial_segments, height_segments, open_ended, theta_start, theta_length]` | A cylinder whose top is a point. |
| `torus[radius, tube_radius, radial_segments, tubular_segments, sweep, theta_start, theta_length]` | About the z axis. `sweep` is three's `arc` (a name of Lory's own: `arc` draws a path's arc here). |
| `torus_knot[radius, tube_radius, tubular_segments, radial_segments, p, q]` | A tube about the (p, q) knot, each ring about the frame three works out from two points a hundredth apart. |
| `capsule[radius, height, cap_segments, radial_segments, height_segments]` | A cylinder between two half spheres, its uvs running by length down the whole. |
| `lathe[points, segments, phi_start, phi_length]` | The points (x, y each) turned about the y axis, their normals across the outline as three works them out. |
| `tube[curve, tubular_segments, radius, radial_segments, closed]` | Rings about a curve's frames; closed, its last ring is its first. |
| `shape[o, curve_segments]`, `shapes[outlines, curve_segments]` | An outline (with holes) flat at z = 0, cut into triangles. `shapes` makes several, a group each. |
| `extrude[outlines, depth, steps, bevel, thickness, size, offset, bevel_segments, curve_segments]` | Outlines stood out along z in steps, their edges bevelled: its faces (group 0), then its sides (group 1), for each outline in turn. No indices; each face its own normal. |
| `extrude_along[outlines, curve, steps, curve_segments]` | Outlines carried along a curve across its frames (x along its normal, y its binormal). There is no bevel along a curve, as in three. |
| `polyhedron[corners, faces, radius, detail]` | Any polyhedron of triangles, each face cut `detail` times along its edges and pushed out onto the sphere. Flat normals at detail 0, round ones after. Its uvs come from where each corner is on the sphere, with three's seam and pole corrections. |
| `tetrahedron`, `octahedron`, `icosahedron`, `dodecahedron` `[radius, detail]` | The regular ones. |

### Curves in space

A curve is a `Curve` record, measured when it is made (the length along it to
each of 200 even steps of its own measure, as three's arc lengths are).

| Function | What it is |
|---|---|
| `line_curve[ax, ay, az, bx, by, bz]` | A straight line. |
| `quadratic_curve[...]`, `cubic_curve[...]` | Bézier curves of three and four points. |
| `spline_curve[points, closed, spline, tension]` | A Catmull-Rom spline through the points: `centripetal` (no cusps, no loops), `chordal`, or `catmullrom` with its tension. An open one reflects its ends past themselves. |
| `helix_curve[radius, height, turns]` | A helix about the y axis, from y = -height / 2. |
| `point_on[curve, t]` | The point `t` of the way along by the curve's own measure. |
| `point_at[curve, u]`, `tangent_at[curve, u]` | The point and the way it goes `u` of the way along by length (three's `getPointAt` and `getTangentAt`). |
| `curve_length[curve]` | Its length as its 200 steps measure it. |
| `frenet_frames[curve, segments, closed]` | `Frames[tangents, normals, binormals]` at `segments` + 1 even steps: each normal turned from the last no more than the curve turns, and, closed, twisted evenly so the last meets the first. |

### Paths and outlines in the plane

| Function | What it is |
|---|---|
| `path_from[x, y]`, `path_through[points]` | A path begun at a point, or one through the points. |
| `move_to`, `line_to`, `quadratic_to`, `cubic_to`, `spline_to` | The pieces three's Path draws, each from where the path is. |
| `arc`, `absolute_arc`, `ellipse`, `absolute_ellipse` | Arcs of circles and ellipses about a centre given from where the path is, or absolutely, clockwise or not, an ellipse turned. A path already drawing is joined to an arc's start by a line, as three joins it. |
| `outline[p]`, `holed[o, p]` | A shape's outline, and the outline with a hole. |
| `path_points[p, divisions]` | The path's points as three's `getPoints` gives them: a line its two ends, an ellipse twice the divisions, a spline the divisions a point, a point the same as the one before it left out. |

A shape's outline is made to go clockwise and its holes the other way, and it
is cut into triangles as earcut cuts it: ears cut off the ring in turn, the
holes bridged in from the leftmost, a ring cleaned, cured of small crossings
and split when no ear is left.

### Lines, and drawing in space

| Function | What it is |
|---|---|
| `edges[g, threshold]` | Where its faces meet at more than `threshold` degrees, and the edges with no face beyond them, two corners each. Corners are told apart to a ten thousandth, as three's EdgesGeometry tells them. |
| `wireframe[g]` | Every edge of every triangle once (an indexed geometry group by group). |
| `space_shape[g]` | The geometry as `space.mesh` takes a shape: its triangles one after another. |
| `lines_drawn[c, g, matrix, width, color]` | A geometry's segments drawn as Lory's flat lines where the scene's eye sees them (call it after `space.scene`), inside the scene's box. A segment reaching behind the eye is left out. |

### Helpers, SVG and fields

| Function | What it is |
|---|---|
| `translated[g, x, y, z]` | The geometry moved (three's `BufferGeometry.translate`). |
| `arrow[c, from, way, length, color, head_length, head_width]` | three's ArrowHelper drawn in the scene: a line and a five-sided cone, turned as three turns them. |
| `svg_paths[d]` | The paths an SVG path's `d` draws, one a subpath: moves, lines (L, H, V), cubic and quadratic curves (C, S, Q, T, their handles mirrored), absolute and relative, closes — as three's SVGLoader reads a path. Elliptical arcs (A) come as lines to their ends. y runs down, as in SVG. |
| `marched[samples, n, size, level]` | The surface where a field sampled on a grid `n` a side (across a cube `size` wide) comes to `level`: marching tetrahedra (each cell cut in six about its long diagonal, no table of cases), each corner placed between grid points in proportion, facing down the field's slope — the surface three's MarchingCubes finds, triangulated otherwise. |
| `ball_field[balls, n, size]` | A field of balls (metaballs: x, y, z and strength each) sampled for `marched`. |

## The example

examples/geometry/main.co, built for the web, shows the shapes one at a time:

- **The shape.** A select of eighteen: the box, plane, circle, ring, sphere,
  cylinder, cone, torus, torus knot, capsule, lathe (a vase), tube, shape (a
  star with a round hole), extrusion (the star, bevelled) and the four
  regular polyhedra.
- **Its numbers.** Sliders for up to four of its main numbers, whole numbers
  where three takes counts: a box's sizes and cuts, a sphere's radius, cuts
  and how far down it goes, a knot's radius, tube, p and q, the polyhedra's
  radius and detail, and a tube's curve (a helix, a closed spline, Bézier
  curves of both kinds, a line) with its radius and cuts.
- **How it is drawn.** Solid, with its edges (where its faces meet at more
  than 20 degrees) and with its wireframe, each a toggle. A note says how
  many vertices and triangles it has.
- **Orbiting.** A drag across the scene turns the camera about the shape
  (`space.orbit`), and the wheel brings it nearer. A drag across the panel
  does not.

The shape is meshed again only when its numbers change; its lines and counts
are kept beside it.

## Behaviours held

The test blocks, each passing under `crow test` and `crow test --compiled`:

| File | Blocks | Truths |
|---|---|---|
| packages/lory/geometry.co | 16 | 105 |
| tests/geometry/reference.co | 1 | 2 truths and an assertion over all 108 cases |
| examples/geometry/main.co | 4 | 15 |

Each behaviour below is a test block in geometry.co:

- A box is six faces of four corners each, a group of its own, cut along each
  face's own two ways.
- A plane faces up the z axis from its top left corner.
- A circle is a fan about its centre of at least three segments. A ring's
  counts follow its two cuts.
- A sphere has one triangle a segment at each pole, its poles' uvs moved half
  a segment. Its upper half keeps both triangles where it is cut off.
- A cylinder is its side, then its top and its bottom; a cone has no top.
- A torus, a knot and a capsule are grids about their tubes.
- A lathe turns its outline about the y axis.
- Curves answer their points by measure and by length. A spline goes through
  its points.
- Frames turn with the curve and no more. A tube's rings are its radius from
  the curve.
- A path is its pieces at even steps, and an arc is joined to it.
- A shape is cut into triangles that cover it and not its holes.
- An extrusion is its two faces, then its sides, each facing out.
- Polyhedra lie on their sphere and are flat until cut.
- Edges are where faces meet at more than the angle; a wireframe has each edge
  once.
- Space holds a geometry as its triangles one after another.
- Lines are drawn where the eye sees them, and not behind it.

The example's blocks (examples/geometry/main.co) hold its first frame (the
knot meshed and drawn with its edges), every shape built from its first
numbers, a slider changing only its shape's numbers, and a drag across the
scene turning it where a drag across the panel does not.

## Parity

- **three's numbers, bit for bit.**
  - `tests/geometry/reference.mjs` runs three.js 0.187.0 (npm `three`, MIT)
    in node on 108 cases: every shape above with several sets of numbers, the
    tube along each kind of curve, shapes with holes and every kind of piece,
    extrusions bevelled, stepped and along curves, the polyhedra of detail 0
    to 3, and edges and wireframes of a dozen shapes. It writes
    `tests/geometry/reference.json`: each case's vertex and index counts, its
    positions, normals and uvs (their float32 bits), its indices and its
    groups.
  - `tests/geometry/reference.co` builds each case in Lory and holds it to
    them. All 162,004 numbers are held: the 130,474 floats are the same
    single-precision floats three wrote, every one to the last bit, and the
    31,530 indices and groups are the same. Where three works from what its
    Float32Arrays hold (its computed normals, its edges, its wireframe), Lory
    rounds to single precision at the same places.
  - To regenerate: `npm install --prefix <scratch>/ref three@0.187.0`, then
    `node tests/geometry/reference.mjs <scratch>/ref/node_modules/three` from
    the repository's root.
- **Both of Crow's modes.** `crow test --compiled` runs plain test blocks in
  the interpreter (it compiles only the apps a block opens), so two programs
  hold the compiled mode:
  - `tests/geometry/reference.co` is also a program. `crow run` (compiled)
    and `crow run -i` each say `108 cases: all 162004 numbers as three made
    them`, so each mode is held to three's numbers on its own.
  - `tests/modes/geometry.co` writes out what the module makes on fixed
    numbers, in 308 lines: each shape's counts, sums, weighted sums and
    bounds, the curves' points, ways and frames, a path's points, and the
    lines a geometry is drawn as. `bash tests/modes.sh crow
    tests/modes/geometry.co` finds the two modes' lines the same.

  The module's, the reference's and the example's blocks pass under
  `crow test` and `crow test --compiled`.
- **The browser.** `examples/geometry` builds for the web
  (`crow build --target web main.co`). `python3 -I tests/geometry/browser.py`,
  run from the repository's root, drives that build in headless Chrome (no
  install: this Mac's Chrome and Python's own library). It checks four
  things, measuring the scene's box each time:
  - the knot is drawn with its edges (42.2% of the box);
  - a drag across the scene turns it (44.5 a pixel apart from before);
  - Tab and ArrowDown choose the next shape, the capsule (53.8 apart);
  - Tab to the Edges toggle and Space take its lines away (2,747 pixels of
    the edges' white, then none).

Two findings of the runs:

- The survey's lead that "extrude of a unit square depth 1 gives 24 vertices
  when no bevel" is three's 36: an extrusion has no indices, so its two faces
  are 12 vertices and its four sides 24.
- About a symmetric closed loop, three's closing twist of a tube's frames
  comes out of rounding alone (its own test of the twist's sign lands about
  1e-17 from 0), so the last bits of a sine decide it. The reference's
  closed loop is not symmetric, and there Lory and three agree to the bit.

## Drawn in space

Space holds a geometry as three hands one to WebGL: `space_form[g]` keeps
its vertices as they are and its triangles by its indices (an element
buffer), `space_segments[g]` its edges or wireframe as lines drawn in the
scene, depth-tested and in both eyes, and `space.colored` a colour a vertex.
Drawn in three's looks (docs/components/space.md) a geometry comes out as
three draws it. `space_shape` and `lines_drawn` remain for Lory's own looks
and flat lines.
